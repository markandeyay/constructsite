## 1. What was added to `qa/a11y.mjs`

WP-16's five checks (axe, computed-background contrast, focus order, reduced
motion, console gate) all stay. Added by this package:

1. **Semantics audit** (section 13.2): h1 count and location, the whole document
   outline, every section's `aria-labelledby` and the tag of what it points at,
   every section numeral's `aria-hidden`, every card title's tag, the skip link's
   href and DOM position.
2. **Text alternative audit** (section 13.2): the plasmid map SVG's role and
   computed accessible name, the 3D stage's role and name, and the static
   still's alt, each rejected if empty, shorter than 40 characters, or matching
   a filename pattern.
3. **Signal-redundancy audit** (section 13.4): PASS and WARN present as text on
   every validation row, the swatch `aria-hidden` so the status is not announced
   twice, and the count of text labels the plasmid map actually renders.
4. **Form audit** (section 10.7): a real `<label for>` per control, no
   placeholder-as-label, `aria-required` on required fields, `aria-live="polite"`
   on the status line, `tabindex="-1"` on the honeypot, and `aria-invalid`
   exercised by submitting the form empty and read back.
5. **Focus indicator and honeypot assertions** inside the focus audit: a visible
   ring at every stop, the skip link first and targeting `#main`, and a hard
   failure if the section 10.6 honeypot ever enters the order.
6. **Overlap audit**: no two laid-out text-bearing elements' bounding boxes
   intersect, at nine scroll positions per viewport, at all three viewports, in
   **both** motion modes. This is the check CLS and opacity cannot make.
7. **Wheel-driven scrolling** replacing `window.scrollTo`, and a settle step.
8. **`?st-debug`**: the page is always opened with WP-10's opt-in query string,
   so the reduced-motion pin and scrub count is read from
   `window.__WP10.ScrollTrigger.getAll()` rather than from a pin-spacer scan.
   The fallback scan is retained for when that global is removed before ship,
   and the gate prints which method it used on every run.

Two harness defects were found and fixed in the process. Both were producing
false failures, and both are recorded because the same traps will catch the next
package:

- **The 36 "focused element is off-screen after focus" failures were the
  harness, not the site.** Chromium scrolls a newly focused control into view;
  under Lenis that scroll is smoothed, so the element is legitimately off screen
  for a few hundred milliseconds. Measured directly: at 1440x900 with motion
  allowed, tab stop 9 reports `top=7439` immediately after the Tab and `top=872`
  900ms later; under reduced motion the same stop is at `top=440` immediately,
  because the scroll is instant. The assertion was measuring the animation. It
  now waits for the scroll to settle before asserting on position.
- **A bare `blur()` does not reset the focus traversal.** Chromium remembers the
  sequential focus navigation starting point, so after the form audit submitted
  the form (which moves focus to the first invalid control) the next traversal
  silently began at tab stop 14 and reported 6 stops instead of 20, while still
  passing. The fix is to focus `body` explicitly, and to run the focus audit
  before the form audit rather than after.

## 4. The plasmid map label: the open finding is CLOSED, and it was not a defect

The orchestrator recorded that on the DEPLOYED page the hero map SVG's
`aria-label` returned an empty string.

**On the built page it does not reproduce.** Measured three ways and they agree:

1. Attribute read: `svg.getAttribute('aria-label')` returns a 533 character
   string.
2. Name computation the way an assistive technology does it, through the
   Chrome DevTools Protocol accessibility tree:
   `.fx-plasmid svg -> role=image name="Circular map of the construct
   pCON-DEMO-01, 5412 base pairs, with 7 annotated features. T7 promoter, a
   promoter, at base 12 to 32 on the forward strand. GFP, a cds, at base 45 to
   762 on the forward strand. ..."` and so on through all seven features with
   name, type, coordinates and strand.
3. axe-core: zero violations, including zero on the SVG.

`role="img"` is on the SVG itself, not on a wrapper. The wrapper
(`div.la-vz-viewer-event-router`) carries `role="presentation"` and no label,
which is correct and is probably what was sampled on the deployed page.

**The most likely explanation for the empty reading, and it is a method trap
worth keeping:** spec section 8.6 mounts seqviz inside a `requestIdleCallback`
after first paint, so for the first several hundred milliseconds there is no SVG
at all, and a query that resolves `document.querySelector('.fx-plasmid svg')` to
nothing, or to a seqviz-internal SVG that has not yet been post-processed, reads
an empty string. The orchestrator's own method note already says a screenshot
taken immediately after load shows an empty map container. The same applies to
reading its label. The gate now waits 2500ms after load before judging anything.

**No cross-WP request to WP-06 is filed.** WP-06 shipped what it reported
shipping. The gate now asserts `role="img"`, a non-empty name and a name of at
least 40 characters at every viewport, so a regression here fails the build.

## 8. Overlap, the check CLS cannot make

No two laid-out elements' bounding boxes intersect. Measured at **nine scroll
positions per viewport, at three viewports, in both motion modes**, which is 54
sampled layouts, comparing up to 154 text-bearing elements pairwise at each.

**Result: 0 intersecting pairs, everywhere.**

Scope and why each exclusion is legitimate rather than convenient:

- Only elements carrying their own **direct** text are compared, and
  ancestor/descendant pairs are skipped. A wrapper containing its child is not
  an overlap.
- Anything inside a `fixed`, `sticky` or `absolute` box is skipped. The site
  header is a fixed overlay by design and is supposed to sit over the page, and
  the section 10.6 honeypot is absolutely positioned at `left: -9999px` on
  purpose. Without this the detector reports the hidden header's own links
  against whatever is under them, at negative `top` values, which is noise.
- Anything with an ancestor at `opacity: 0` is skipped. **This one was found by
  measurement, not assumed.** The first version of the detector reported
  `span.sec-validation__word` intersecting `span.sec-validation__reason` by
  30x10px at every viewport. Checked directly against the revealed state: the
  word's box is `top 586.7, bottom 604.7` and the reason's is `top 608.7`, a
  clean 4px gap. The 10px "overlap" is the `.reveal` primitive's
  `translateY(14px)` pre-reveal offset minus that 4px gap, measured on an element
  still at opacity 0 that no user has ever seen. Reporting it would have been a
  false positive; suppressing it without checking would have been worse.

One device pixel of tolerance is allowed, because sub-pixel line boxes touch
legitimately.

This is the check that would have caught WP-09a's hero, where the headline
overlapped the kicker by 12.8px at rest while CLS read exactly 0.0000 and every
opacity assertion passed, because parallax is a transform and transforms are
excluded from layout-shift scoring by design.

## 12. Lighthouse Accessibility: the real number, and the one thing that costs it

Spec section 12 targets 98 or better. `@lhci/cli` 0.14 is installed and was used.
Desktop preset, against the same preview build.

| Run | Settings | Score | Failing audits |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | default | **96** | `color-contrast`, one node |
| 2 | default | **100** | none |
| 3 | default | **96** | `color-contrast`, one node |
| 4 | default | **96** | `color-contrast`, one node |
| 5 | `pauseAfterLoadMs=6000` | **100** | none |

**Every failing run names exactly one node, and it is always the same node:**

```
section#hero > div.container > div.sec-hero__copy > p.micro
<p class="micro sec-hero__status reveal is-revealed" style="will-change: transform, opacity;">
LIVE . OPEN SOURCE . MIT LICENSED
```

with the effective foreground reported as `#6f777f` (4.31:1) on one run,
`#747c84` (4.02:1) on another and `#93999e` (2.73:1) on a third. Three different
foregrounds for one element across three runs is the diagnosis: **Lighthouse is
sampling the element mid-fade.** The declared colour is `#656E77`
(`--ink-faint`), the settled measured ratio on `--paper` is **4.93:1**, and the
element passes AA the moment its opacity transition finishes. The gate's own
contrast audit, which settles first, measures 4.93:1 and passes.

Why this element and no other: spec section 7.1 fades the hero status line in at
`t = 2.40s`, which is squarely inside the window Lighthouse samples, and
`--ink-faint` on `--paper` has only 0.43 of margin over the 4.5 threshold, so
any opacity below about 0.90 drops it under. Nothing else on the page combines
those two properties in the first seconds of load. The section 06 PDB credit is
tighter still at 4.56:1 and also fades, but it is far enough down the page that
Lighthouse never samples it mid-transition.

**This is a genuine tension between two parts of the spec, not a mistake by
anyone.** Section 7.1 asks for the status line to fade in and section 5.2 fixes
its colour at `--ink-faint`; section 12 asks for a Lighthouse Accessibility score
of 98 or better, and Lighthouse scores a single sampled frame. A cross-WP request
is filed against `src/sections/hero.ts` with two candidate fixes, in section 13
below. **No file was edited: this package owns `qa/a11y.mjs` and nothing else.**

Honest summary for the record: **the site meets WCAG AA in its settled state at
every viewport, and scores 96 on most default Lighthouse runs and 100 when the
fade has completed before sampling.** The spec section 12 target of 98 is
therefore not reliably met today, for that one reason.
