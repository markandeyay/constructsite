**The janked-frame budget, settled.** WP-15 measured 9.99% and validated it
against a blank control reading 17.1ms median and 0.00% janked. WP-07-fix
rebuilt that control and got 50.65 to 56.86% janked, and concluded the budget
was not assessable. WP-17 re-ran both control shapes interleaved with the site,
same flags, same 6x CPU throttle, same wheel-driven sweep:

| page | median frame | janked > 20ms | long tasks | frames in sweep |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| blank control, no per-frame work | 30.1 / 30.5 / 30.3 ms | 73.4 / 73.2 / 74.1 % | 1 / 0 / 0 | ~395 |
| blank control, one trivial transform per frame | 1.4 / 1.3 ms | 0.07 / 0.05 % | 0 / 1 | 5856 / 6041 |

| the site, 375x812 | 10.1 / 10.2 ms | 27.4 / 20.5 % | 6 / 8, none over 200ms | 678 / 712 |

The disagreement is explained. **A blank page that paints nothing has its rAF
throttled** to roughly vsync or half vsync, so both earlier controls were
measuring idle throttling, not a jank floor: WP-15's landed at 17.1ms, under
the 20ms threshold, and read 0%; WP-07-fix's landed at 26 to 30ms, over it, and
read 56%. Neither is a floor. Give the control one trivial transform per frame
and the same machine, same flags, same throttle, same wheel sweep delivers
**1.3 to 1.4ms median and 0.05% janked over roughly 6000 frames**.

So the budget IS assessable right now, and the verdict is **MISSED**: 20.5% to
34.8% at 1440x900 and 20.5% to 27.4% at 375x812 against 2%, with the machine's
floor at 0.05%. The site produces 450 to 710 frames where the floor produces
6000, so the main thread is the limiter. WP-07-fix's diagnosis of the 375px
case, that it is browser paint of the large static hero SVG rather than this
site's JavaScript, remains the best-evidenced explanation and its three
candidate remedies (`contain: paint`, an explicit composited layer, or
`content-visibility: auto` with a matching `contain-intrinsic-size` applied
after the draw animation) are still unmeasured against each other.

**The long-task budget.** At 375x812 there were zero tasks over 200ms in both
sweeps. At 1440x900 `qa/perf.mjs` recorded two: 690ms starting at 9993.3ms and
1731ms starting at 10793ms. The structure section came within 1.5 viewport
heights at 9611.6ms, so BOTH fall inside the 3000ms one-time-init window and
both are 3Dmol init work; the harness excludes exactly one of them by design.
This reproduces WP-07-fix's exclusion-granularity finding. The honest reading:
the budget fails as scored, the failure is one init counted as two tasks rather
than scroll work, and the correct remedy is to fix the exclusion granularity in

## 3. THE OVERLAP QUESTION

The specific failure mode named in the brief, a headline overlapping a kicker
while CLS reads 0.0000 and every opacity assertion passes, was re-tested
directly, because transforms are excluded from layout-shift scoring by design
and neither CLS nor opacity can see it.

`qa/a11y.mjs`, re-run by WP-17, performs a pairwise bounding-box intersection
across up to 154 text-bearing elements at nine scroll positions per viewport at
375x812, 900x1200 and 1440x900 in both motion modes, 54 layouts in total:
**0 intersecting pairs**.

Its exclusions are disclosed and one of them matters: it skips fixed, sticky
and absolutely positioned elements, and section 03's visual column is
absolutely positioned while staged, so the a11y gate never compares it.

WP-17 therefore ran an independent probe that does NOT exclude absolutely
positioned elements, over 11 scroll stops per viewport at the same three
widths in both motion modes, with a positive control that nudges the hero lede
60px upward to prove the probe can see an intersection.

Results. The probe works: the control run, with the hero lede pulled 60px up,
recorded **8 intersecting pairs**, the worst 94x24px, so it can see the exact
bug class that CLS and opacity miss.

The full 11-stop sweep recorded hits, and they sort into two groups. The first
group is an artifact of the probe and not a defect: the fixed header's anchors
pass over page content as you scroll, which is what a fixed header does. Those
elements are themselves `position: static` inside a fixed parent, so the naive
filter kept them. A second, tightened run that excludes anything with a fixed
or sticky ANCESTOR resolves the picture completely:

| viewport | intersecting pairs | elements compared | all inside the plasmid map? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 375x812 | 3 | 22 | yes, 3 of 3 |
| 900x1200 | 3 | 27 | yes, 3 of 3 |
| 1440x900 | **0** | 47 | n/a |

So **this site's own laid-out DOM has zero overlapping boxes at all three
widths in both motion modes.** The hero parallax fix is holding and no other
section has the defect.

What remains is real and visible, and it is inside seqviz's own rendered SVG at
the smaller map sizes:

| pair | overlap | where |
|---|---|---|
| `tspan: pCON-DEMO-01` over `text: +1` | 12 x 11 px | centre name block against a strand marker |
| `text: 1800` over `textPath: AmpR` | 14 x 11 px | coordinate tick against an arc label |
| `text: 3600` over `textPath: KanR` | 19 x 9 px | coordinate tick against an arc label |

All three occur at 375 and at 900 and none at 1440. Looking at the 900px render
confirms it: the construct name block `pCON-DEMO-01 / 5412 bp` is drawn against
the BOTTOM edge of the ring rather than at its centre, sitting on the ring
stroke and over the `2700` tick. WP-11 logged this at 900 and 1024 as a
renderer placement quirk that costs no CLS and no overflow, which is true, but
it is text over text in the hero visual on a phone and a tablet and it is the
one thing on the page that looks unfinished. Owner: `src/fx/plasmid.ts`
(WP-06). The fix is a size floor for the circular viewer below 901px, or
suppressing seqviz's own centre label and drawing the construct name in the
wrapper where its position is ours to control.

cost of the mobile order and worth knowing before a phone demo.
